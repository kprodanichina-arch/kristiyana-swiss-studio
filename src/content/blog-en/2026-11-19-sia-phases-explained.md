If you work on Swiss projects, sooner or later someone will ask whether you are "in phase 51 yet". This article explains the SIA phases in plain English, shows how they roughly map to the RIBA Plan of Work 2020 stages and the German HOAI work stages, and sets out what that means when you plan – or outsource – technical design on a Swiss project.

We are a small architecture and BIM practice working remotely for offices in Switzerland, Germany and Austria. Mixing up phase numbers is one of the most common sources of misunderstanding we see in cross-border teams, so here is the version we wish we had been given.

## What are the SIA phases?

The Swiss Society of Engineers and Architects (SIA) publishes SIA 112 "Modell Bauplanung" (2014 edition). It splits a building project into six phases with numbered sub-phases and works as a common language between clients and consultants. The SIA fee and service regulations – for architects, SIA 102 – build on these sub-phases. Like all SIA regulations, they only become binding when the contract says so.

The sub-phases you will meet most often on building projects:

- **Phase 2 Preliminary studies:** 21 project definition and feasibility study; 22 selection procedure (e.g. competition)
- **Phase 3 Design:** 31 preliminary design (*Vorprojekt*), 32 design project (*Bauprojekt*), 33 building permit procedure
- **Phase 4 Tender:** 41 tender, bid comparison, contract award
- **Phase 5 Construction:** 51 construction documentation (*Ausführungsprojekt*), 52 construction (*Ausführung*), 53 commissioning and close-out
- **Phase 6 Operation:** operation and maintenance

## How do SIA phases compare to RIBA stages and HOAI?

There is no exact one-to-one match, because each framework cuts the work differently. As a working aid for proposals, programmes and conversations with partner offices, we use this approximate mapping:

- **SIA 21 / 31** ≈ RIBA Stages 0–2 (Strategic Definition, Preparation and Briefing, Concept Design) ≈ HOAI LPH 1–2
- **SIA 32 Bauprojekt** ≈ RIBA Stage 3 Spatial Coordination ≈ HOAI LPH 3
- **SIA 33 permit procedure** ≈ HOAI LPH 4; under RIBA, the timing of the planning application depends on the project strategy
- **SIA 41 tender and award** ≈ HOAI LPH 6–7; RIBA has no separate tender stage – timing depends on the procurement route
- **SIA 51 Ausführungsprojekt** ≈ RIBA Stage 4 Technical Design ≈ HOAI LPH 5
- **SIA 52 / 53** ≈ RIBA Stages 5–6 (Manufacturing and Construction, Handover) ≈ HOAI LPH 8
- **SIA phase 6** ≈ RIBA Stage 7 Use, partly HOAI LPH 9

Treat this as a translation aid, not a standard. What you actually owe is defined by your contract and its scope of services.

## Why does SIA phase 51 come after the tender?

This is the practical difference that catches people out. Under the HOAI, technical design (LPH 5) comes before tender preparation (LPH 6), so bills of quantities are ideally based on the technical design – although in practice the two often overlap.

In the SIA model, the tender (41) comes before the Ausführungsprojekt (51). Contractors are invited on the basis of the Bauprojekt plus tender drawings, details and specifications. The construction documentation follows the award: ETH Zurich's "Navigator zum Bauprozess" notes that once the contract with the contractor is signed, the scope is defined and the contractor's know-how feeds into the working and detail drawings. Shop drawings are checked and released in this phase.

UK readers will recognise the pattern. The RIBA Plan of Work 2020 overview says that specialist subcontractor design belongs in Stage 4, and that on a traditional route the design team's work should be substantially complete before the building contract is signed, with specialist subcontractor design completed after the contractor's appointment. Swiss practice simply puts a larger share of the architect's construction documentation after the award.

What this means for the people drawing:

- **Less lead time:** working and detail drawings are often produced shortly before or alongside the start on site. A drawing issue programme is essential.
- **More interfaces:** contractor information – systems, products, installation drawings – goes straight into the drawings. Changes must be tracked carefully.
- **Different vocabulary:** *Werkplanung*, *Ausführungsplanung* and *Ausführungsprojekt* are often used interchangeably. Agree whether 1:20 to 1:1 details are included.

## Outsourcing technical design on a Swiss project: what to agree

SIA phase 51/52 is where workload peaks – and where external support pays off. Before you hand work over to an external team, put these points in writing:

1. **Phase reference in both systems:** for example "SIA sub-phase 51 (broadly RIBA Stage 4 / HOAI LPH 5)", so everyone means the same thing.
2. **Drawing list:** which plans, sections, elevations, details and façade drawings, at which scale and in which order.
3. **Inputs:** Bauprojekt, permit conditions, award results, contractor information, consultant models.
4. **Drawing standards:** title blocks, layers, pens, dimensioning. In Switzerland, SIA 400 "Planbearbeitung im Hochbau" is often the reference – or your own office standard.
5. **Data exchange:** native Archicad files (PLN), Teamwork via BIMcloud, or IFC for consultants – plus PDF drawings as the issued set.
6. **Review and release:** who checks drawings, who releases shop drawings, how revisions are marked.

Typical mistakes we see: construction documentation starts from the Bauprojekt even though the award changed the systems; revisions are not logged in the drawing register; and the IFC exchange is only tested once the first drawings are due.

## Are the SIA regulations changing?

The SIA is revising its fee and service regulations SIA 102, 103, 105 and 108. According to espazium, the public consultation ran from 25 November 2024 to 28 February 2025, and approval by the SIA delegates was planned for autumn 2026. The basic structure is to be kept, but services will be assigned to functions as well as disciplines.

We could not reliably confirm at the time of writing whether the revised regulations have been approved or when they take effect. Check the current status with the SIA and state in your contract which edition applies. We will update this article once confirmed information is available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SIA phase 51 the same as RIBA Stage 4?

Broadly, yes – both cover the information needed to build. The main difference is timing: SIA 51 usually follows the contract award and builds on contractor information. In contracts, describe the drawing list rather than only naming the phase.

### Does the HOAI or the RIBA Plan of Work apply in Switzerland?

Neither applies by default. Swiss projects are usually contracted under SIA regulations or bespoke agreements. Which rules apply depends on the individual contract – take legal advice if in doubt.

### Can an external team take on only part of SIA phase 51?

Yes. Often only part of 51/52 is outsourced – for example façade details, working drawings for selected floors, or updating drawings during construction. What matters are clear boundaries and an agreed data exchange.

## Our conclusion

SIA phases, RIBA stages and HOAI work stages describe similar work at a different rhythm. The key difference is the order of tender and construction documentation – and with it the time pressure under which Swiss working and detail drawings are produced. Agreeing phase references, drawing list, standards and data exchange up front avoids most misunderstandings in cross-border teams.
